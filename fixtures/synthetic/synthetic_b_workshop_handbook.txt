SYNTHETIC TEST FIXTURE B - "Handbook of the Fictional Lantern Workshop"

SYNTHETIC FIXTURE: invented text for software testing and demos. It is not a real
author, not a real publication, and not a Marxist-Leninist source.

Chapter 1. Investigate before you decide

A member of the lantern workshop who has not investigated has no right to speak about
what the workshop should do. The handbook insists that facts be gathered first: who
makes the lanterns, which steps are slow, and what the customers actually say. An
observation is what was seen or heard; an inference is what the investigator concludes
from it, and the two must be kept apart in every report.

Chapter 2. Correcting mistakes

When the workshop shipped lanterns with cracked glass, the handbook records that the
first explanation (careless workers) was dropped after the facts showed a faulty crate
design. The handbook says a mistake is useful when it is examined openly and the
explanation is revised in light of the evidence.

Chapter 3. Apprentices and delegation

An experienced lantern maker should teach an apprentice by letting the apprentice try a
task, then reviewing the result together. Delegation means handing over a real task with
clear limits and then assessing the outcome fairly, using the same standard for every
apprentice. The handbook calls this the cheapest way to grow capable makers.

Chapter 4. Choosing what to do first

The handbook says that when many problems press at once, the workshop should name the
main constraint, the one problem whose solution most loosens the others, and revisit that
choice whenever the facts change.
